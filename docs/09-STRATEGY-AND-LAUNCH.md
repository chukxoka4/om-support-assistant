## The AI Adoption Rubric — the spine of everything

Awesome Motive assesses Support / CS staff against a four-tier **AI Adoption
Rubric**: **Unacceptable → Capable → Adaptive → Transformative**. The full
authoritative rubric text lives in the skill (below); the short version:

- **Capable** — uses AI to draft, edit, research, summarise customer history,
  spot upsell, translate to the customer's level.
- **Adaptive** — systematised workflows cutting response time 50%+; pattern
  analysis to flag at-risk customers; expansion at scale; proactive outreach
  drafts; tracks CS metrics (expansion revenue, churn prevented); **maintains a
  prompt library across scenarios**.
- **Transformative** — org-wide opportunity prioritisation; outreach playbooks
  with measurable revenue lift; AI health-scoring / early-warning; trains the
  team; feeds synthesised insights to product & marketing.

**This whole extension is the agent's evidence for moving up the rubric.** The
rubric assessment "started from my Q1 review" — it's the frame the agent is
graded in, and the reason the tool leans on a learning prompt library, outcome
metrics, the customer-health chip, and the weekly friction digest. Every design
choice traces back to *"which rubric criterion does this satisfy?"*

**Current placement** (per [06-REVIEW-PLAN.md](06-REVIEW-PLAN.md) and the
`ai-adoption-rubric-support` skill): solid **Capable** across the board;
**Adaptive** on measured-baseline, personalised templates, and library-learns;
a **Transformative** foothold via the customer chip (account intelligence). The
missing Adaptive/Transformative pieces are proactive outreach (F3) and
cross-ticket synthesis / voice-of-customer (F4).

## The Slack soft-launch (2026-05-08 afternoon)

After building Report & Suggestions v2, the agent turned to **soft-launching
the extension on a Slack channel** — showcasing it against the AI Adoption
Rubric, framed as follow-through from the Q1 review, with a screenshot of a
sample weekly report. Goal: visibility with the supervisor / team, positioning
the tool as rubric evidence.

**What the agent wanted the post to be:**
- A short intro framing the work against the Q1 review and the rubric.
- A tight, feature-by-feature mapping of the extension to rubric levels
  (Capable / Adaptive / Transformative) — *"the biggest and most impactful
  features that match the rubric points,"* as a clean, abbreviated list, not a
  wall of text.
- Visuals of **the extension itself** (side panel), not only the report.

**"Claude Design" (a.k.a. the Anthropic design/artifact tooling).** The agent
wanted to use it to produce annotated visuals / an autoplay animation of the
side panel + report instead of static screenshots, and to explore turning an
autoplay HTML into a shareable video. This thread was **exploratory and did not
land a finished artefact** in this repo. See
[OPEN-THREADS.md OT-14](OPEN-THREADS.md#ot-14).

> **Working-style note for future AI (learn from this).** During this thread
> the assistant initially claimed not to know "Claude Design" and pushed back
> instead of researching it. The user's reaction was sharp: *"I find it
> disappointing you do not know about Claude Design… can't you even search?
> your company announced it, people use it but you don't know it… do better."*
> This is the exact failure the global rule in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` targets:
> **search the docs before declaring something unknown or impossible.** If the
> user references a tool/feature you don't recognise, look it up — don't argue.

## What a future owner should carry forward

1. **The rubric is the "why."** When prioritising, ask which rubric tier a
   change advances. Proactive outreach (F3) and cross-ticket synthesis (F4) are
   the next real rubric unlocks — everything else is polish.
2. **Erica's rewrites are the gold-standard training signal.** Protect that
   branch of the learning loop; don't let refactors quietly break
   `managerial_rewrite` attribution.
3. **The May 1 reporting pitch and the weekly digest are a standing
   commitment** to the supervisor, not a one-off. Don't casually change the
   digest format.
4. **Search before you claim ignorance.** The Claude Design episode is the
   cautionary tale.
